Credit Review Checklist: 9 Ratios, with Target and Watch Limits

A preliminary credit review doesn't need a black-box score. It needs a short list of ratios, limits you can defend, and an honest status for each one. Here is a practical checklist.

The 9 indicators

GroupIndicatorFormulaBetter when
LiquidityCurrent ratioCurrent assets ÷ Current liabilitiesHigher
Quick ratio(Current assets − Inventory) ÷ Current liabilitiesHigher
Debt serviceInterest coverageEBIT ÷ Interest expenseHigher
Debt service coverage (simplified)EBITDA ÷ (Interest + Scheduled principal)Higher

PerformanceEBITDA marginEBITDA ÷ RevenueHigher

Revenue growthRevenue ÷ Prior-year revenue − 1Higher

Set two limits per ratio: Target and Watch

For each indicator, set a Target (comfortable) and a Watch level (needs attention). Each period then gets one of three statuses:

  • Within target: at or better than Target.
  • On watch: between Target and Watch.
  • Outside target: worse than Watch.

Limits depend on your institution's credit policy, the industry and the size of the borrower, so set them yourself rather than copying generic benchmarks. Write down why you chose each limit.

The rule most templates miss: "not assessed"

If a ratio can't be calculated honestly, its status must say so. Examples: a required input is blank, the balance sheet doesn't balance, equity is negative, or there is no prior year to compare against for growth. Mark it not assessed and give the reason. Never let it count as "within target".

Why it matters: a review that shows "all 9 within target" while 2 indicators were silently built on bad data is worse than no review at all.

Summarise per period

  • Number of indicators within target, on watch, outside target and not assessed.
  • Number of open data-check issues (failed, warning or not assessed).
  • A one-line review prompt, for example "All 9 within target" or "Review required: 1 not assessed".
Show each status in words and a symbol, not colour alone, so the review survives printing in black and white and is readable for colour-blind colleagues.

What this is not

A ratio checklist is decision support. It does not replace management quality, collateral, cash-flow projections, sector outlook or your institution's credit policy.
