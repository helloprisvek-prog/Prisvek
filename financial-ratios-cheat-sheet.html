Financial Ratios Cheat Sheet: 24 Formulas, What They Mean, and the Red Flags

This page collects the ratios you need for financial statement analysis and credit review. Each one comes with its formula and a one-line meaning, and each group ends with the red flag analysts look for. A printable one-page PDF version is free at the end.

Step 0: check the data before you calculate a single ratio

A ratio is only as good as the statements behind it. Run these three checks first:

  1. The balance sheet balances. Total assets = total liabilities + equity, in every period, to the unit. A small gap usually means a missing line or a sign error.
  2. Retained earnings roll forward. Closing RE ≈ opening RE + net income − dividends (± other adjustments). If they don't, profit, dividends or a reclassification is missing.
  3. Current-year profit is in equity. If the balance-sheet gap equals net income, the year's profit was never posted to equity, and every equity-based ratio for that year (ROE, debt-to-equity) is wrong.
Use average balances (opening + closing ÷ 2) for ratios that mix the income statement with the balance sheet, such as ROA, ROE, turnover and days ratios, whenever two periods exist.

Liquidity ratios: can the company pay the next 12 months?

RatioFormulaWhat it tells you
Current ratioCurrent assets ÷ Current liabilitiesShort-term cover. Below 1.0× means current debts exceed current assets.
Quick ratio(Current assets − Inventory) ÷ Current liabilitiesCover without selling stock.
Cash ratioCash & equivalents ÷ Current liabilitiesThe strictest test: cash only.
Red flag: a quick ratio far below the current ratio, so liquidity depends on selling inventory.

Profitability ratios: does it earn enough?

RatioFormulaWhat it tells you
Gross marginGross profit ÷ RevenuePricing power and direct-cost control.
Operating marginEBIT ÷ RevenueProfit from the core business.
Net marginNet income ÷ RevenueWhat is left after interest and tax.
ROANet income ÷ Average total assetsHow well all assets earn.
ROENet income ÷ Average equityReturn to shareholders.
Red flag: ROE rising while margins fall. The "improvement" comes from more debt, not a better business. See DuPont analysis.

Efficiency and working-capital ratios

RatioFormulaWhat it tells you
Asset turnoverRevenue ÷ Average total assetsSales generated per unit of assets.
DSO (days)Average receivables ÷ Revenue × 365How long customers take to pay.
DIO (days)Average inventory ÷ COGS × 365How long stock sits before it is sold.
DPO (days)Average payables ÷ COGS × 365How long the company takes to pay suppliers.
Cash conversion cycleDSO + DIO − DPODays of funding the business needs.

Worked example: cash conversion cycle

Revenue 73,000; COGS 43,800; average receivables 10,000; average inventory 7,200; average payables 4,800.

  • DSO = 10,000 ÷ 73,000 × 365 = 50 days
  • DIO = 7,200 ÷ 43,800 × 365 = 60 days
  • DPO = 4,800 ÷ 43,800 × 365 = 40 days
  • CCC = 50 + 60 − 40 = 70 days of funding needed
Red flag: receivables growing faster than revenue. Sales may be booked but not collected.

Leverage and debt-service ratios

RatioFormulaWhat it tells you
Debt-to-equityTotal debt ÷ Total equityFinancing mix.
Debt ratioTotal liabilities ÷ Total assetsShare of assets funded by creditors.
Net debt / EBITDA(Total debt − Cash) ÷ EBITDAYears of earnings needed to repay debt.
Interest coverageEBIT ÷ Interest expenseBelow about 1.5× is usually read as weak. Covenants vary.
Equity multiplierAverage total assets ÷ Average equityThe leverage factor in DuPont.
Red flag: negative equity. Debt-to-equity becomes meaningless and should not be reported as a number.

Cash-flow ratios: is the profit real?

RatioFormulaWhat it tells you
Cash conversionOperating cash flow ÷ Net incomePersistently below 1.0× deserves an explanation.
Free cash flowOperating cash flow − CapexCash left after investment.
OCF to debtOperating cash flow ÷ Total debtAbility to service debt from operations.
Capex to depreciationCapex ÷ DepreciationBelow 1.0× for years may mean under-investment.
Red flag: profit up and operating cash flow down, two years in a row.

DuPont: why did ROE change?

ROE = Net margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier. The 5-step version splits net margin into tax burden (net income ÷ pre-tax income), interest burden (pre-tax income ÷ EBIT) and EBIT margin. If the factors don't multiply back to ROE, the inputs are inconsistent. Full worked example →
